\documentclass[10pt, conference]{IEEEtran} \usepackage[utf8]{inputenc} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{graphicx} \usepackage{booktabs} \usepackage{hyperref} \title{P!ng: An Optical Alcohol Detection Framework Based on Refractive Index Deviation and BLE Integration} \author{ \IEEEauthorblockN{Technical Research Division} \IEEEauthorblockA{Project P!ng Development Team \\ Email: engineering@ping-detection.tech} } \begin{document} \maketitle \begin{abstract} Alcohol detection systems are critical for vehicular safety. While absorption-based spectroscopy in the visible spectrum (650 nm) is limited due to the transparency of ethanol, this paper proposes P!ng: a system that utilizes Refractive Index (RI) deviation as a proxy for concentration measurement. By monitoring the spatial displacement of a 650 nm laser beam through a liquid medium via a BPW40 phototransistor, the system detects variations in ethanol concentration. Data is processed using a Multi-Sample Decision Algorithm on an ESP32 microcontroller and transmitted via Bluetooth Low Energy. This approach offers a low-cost, non-chemical alternative for real-time monitoring. \end{abstract} \section{Introduction} Traditional alcohol sensors rely on electrochemical reactions that degrade over time. Optical sensing provides a non-contact alternative. However, ethanol absorption peaks reside primarily in the Mid-Infrared range (3.3 $\mu$m). In the visible spectrum, detection must rely on the refractive properties of the mixture. This paper explores the feasibility of using beam deviation caused by the refractive index of ethanol-water mixtures as a detection mechanism. \section{Theoretical Framework} \subsection{Refractive Index of Mixtures} The refractive index ($n$) of a water-ethanol mixture varies predictably with concentration. Water has an RI of 1.333, while pure ethanol is approximately 1.361. The refractive index of the mixture ($n_{mix}$) can be modeled as: \begin{equation} n_{mix} \approx \phi_{w}n_{w} + \phi_{e}n_{e} \end{equation} where $\phi$ represents the volume fraction of the components. \subsection{Beam Deviation and Snell's Law} As the laser passes through the sample cell, the change in $n_{mix}$ causes a shift in the exit angle of the beam according to Snell's Law ($n_1 \sin \theta_1 = n_2 \sin \theta_2$). This spatial shift results in the beam partially moving off the active area of the photodiode, leading to a measurable drop in voltage ($V_{out}$). \section{System Design} \subsection{Hardware Layer} The system utilizes a 650 nm Laser Diode and a BPW40 phototransistor in a fixed-path alignment. The ESP32 serves as the central processing unit, utilizing its 12-bit ADC for high-resolution signal ingestion. \subsection{Software Logic} To prevent false positives from ambient light, the system employs a Multi-Sample Decision Algorithm (MSDA): \begin{itemize} \item Sample Rate: 10Hz (100ms intervals). \item Confidence Threshold: 80\% (8/10 samples must meet criteria). \item Communication: BLE GATT notification for real-time mobile alerts. \end{itemize} \section{Experimental Results} Testing was conducted with ethanol concentrations from 0\% to 40\%. \begin{table}[h] \centering \caption{Refractive Signal Attenuation Data} \begin{tabular}{@{}ccc@{}} \toprule \textbf{Ethanol (\%) } & \textbf{Refractive Index (est.)} & \textbf{Mean ADC Output} \\ \midrule 0 (Control) & 1.333 & 2450 \\ 10 & 1.340 & 2180 \\ 20 & 1.347 & 1850 \\ 40 & 1.354 & 1420 \\ \bottomrule \end{tabular} \end{table} The inverse correlation demonstrates that as the refractive index increases, the beam deviation increases, reducing the light intensity captured by the sensor. \section{Future Scope and Improvements} \subsection{Signal Modulation} To eliminate ambient interference, future iterations will pulse the laser at 1 kHz. A software-based bandpass filter will isolate this frequency, ensuring the system remains functional in direct sunlight. \subsection{VCSEL Integration} Transitioning to a 940 nm VCSEL (Near-Infrared) will allow the system to leverage overtone absorption bands, increasing chemical specificity while maintaining a low-cost profile. \section{Conclusion} The P!ng system demonstrates that while 650 nm light is not absorbed by ethanol, the change in refractive index provides a robust, measurable signal for detection. This hardware-software fusion creates a scalable foundation for non-invasive safety sensors in the automotive IoT ecosystem. \section*{References} \small \begin{enumerate} \item A.
